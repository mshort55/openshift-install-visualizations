# OpenShift Bare-metal Installation Journey

The default page of the learning app for Red Hat engineers exploring the installation customer experience on bare metal, using the Agent-based Installer as the main path. A vertical timeline maps eight stages across preparation, installation and post-installation. Stages expand into steps, customer problems and candidate improvements. It includes 50 topics, 21 representative problem records and 15 engineering hypotheses.

Run and build the combined app using the [root instructions](../../../README.md). This page owns the timeline and its three perspectives. The shared app owns the header, page navigation, sharing and browser history. Choices is a separate page with contextual links from deployment planning and the Agent-based method explanation.

The timeline has three perspectives: **How it works**, **Customer problems**, and **Improvement opportunities**. Each uses the same stages in the same order. Open a stage to reveal its step and substep headings, then a step to reveal its content inline. Nested steps expose deeper explanations. Each customer problem is an inline disclosure: its title, evidence context and summary remain visible, and its chevron expands the evidence, impact, outcome and limitations beneath it. Opportunities use the same inline disclosures, expanding their basis, existing capabilities and proposed test. Multiple problems or opportunities can stay open. Switching perspectives preserves the expanded sections and their position on screen.

Groups—**Networking and shared services**, **Content, configuration and media**, **Enable workload storage**, and **Prepare platform operations**—introduce their task subsections without repeating a parent process or context block. In **How it works**, individual tasks show a summary, the main process, and **Outcome, people and context**, including the customer’s finish line. These context panels are unavailable in the other perspectives. Nested step headings appear separately beneath the parent's explanation, so a substep can be visible while its parent's explanation stays closed. The other two perspectives replace the main process with relevant findings. Customer problems and opportunities appear only at their assigned step, without repeated cards in related steps. Topics marked `kind: "group"` use the grouping layout; other topics use the task layout.

The initial view shows a compact stage overview. One toolbar above the timeline provides three actions: **Show all steps** reveals every stage, step and nested substep heading and closes all explanations; **Expand all details** opens every step explanation, individual problem disclosure, context panel, installation-method comparison and full problem or opportunity list; **Collapse all** returns to the stage overview. In **Customer problems**, the first action becomes **Show All Problems**: it expands only stages containing assigned problems and steps with their own problem cards, showing each full problem card once while leaving individual problem details closed. Stages without assigned problems remain collapsed, including when the action follows a selected step or **Expand all details**. In **Improvement opportunities**, **Show all opportunities** reveals each summary once at its assigned step, and **Expand all opportunities** opens every opportunity disclosure. **Collapse all** returns to the stage overview in every perspective. These actions replace the current expansion state, including any selected detail. Individual stage and step controls remain available. After Show all steps, opening or closing an explanation keeps the surrounding outline visible. Expansion lasts while exploring the three perspectives; search and shared links reveal the selected stage, topic or record without requiring its parent explanations to open.

A sticky perspective selector stays available while reading. Browser Back restores the earlier selection, scroll position and expanded record list. Search opens the matching step or record in the timeline; a problem or opportunity result expands its disclosure. Shared record links do the same, and closing a selected record keeps its step open. Share view copies the current stage, topic, perspective and record, with a manual-copy fallback when clipboard access is unavailable. Existing topic and record links still work. Previously shared problem and opportunity links from other steps still open the record at its assigned step. Links to merged storage behavior findings open the combined storage account; retired problem links open the corresponding acceptance, recovery, lifecycle or research guidance in How it works.

The timeline shows typical progression. Local notes explain parallel preparation, retry loops and continuing operations. Installer methods are labeled as alternatives rather than successive tasks. The same vertical layout adapts to mobile screens.

The **Plan the deployment** step provides an optional, collapsed comparison of four installation methods. Selecting a method reveals its distinct process steps for orientation; the timeline continues to follow the standard local Agent CLI workflow. **How it works** includes expandable sections for cluster and host configuration, full/minimal ISO and PXE boot options, and the separate local-web-interface workflow that installs without an external registry. **Customer problems** and **Improvement opportunities** show the shared findings once at the approach level. Existing method links and search results open the corresponding comparison choice. Comparison expansion is independent of outcome/context expansion and participates in Expand all details. Show all steps keeps the optional method comparison closed; installation methods remain alternatives rather than successive journey steps. Commands appear within the relevant expanded process steps. DNS preparation, external-record timing, and access checks are consolidated in **DNS and cluster access**; links to the former DNS subsections open this section.

Content lives in `data/topics.ts` and `data/evidence.ts`. The ordered stage groups and their perspective summaries live in `data/stages.ts`. Every detailed topic belongs to one stage through its existing parent hierarchy. Each topic has an outcome, owners and a scoped explanation. Customer problems describe the situation, consequence, reported outcome and limits of the available evidence without naming customers. Their short date/platform context appears on the summary card. Expanded cards list anonymous examples with a separate reported or study-report date, occurrence timing, evidence basis and limitation. Disputed call dates and unknown timing remain explicit. Dates are searchable; a report date is never silently used as an occurrence date. Each problem has one `topic` for its card. Former `relatedTopics` associations are retained only to resolve previously shared links; they do not affect displayed counts or expansion. The first entry in an opportunity’s nonempty `topics` list owns its card; other entries resolve related and published links. Opportunities use a typed `basis` to distinguish links to reported problems from links to required tasks or acceptance checks, and explain existing capabilities, an intervention, an investigation and useful observations. Neither the displayed order nor the number of records is a severity or prevalence score.

Pre-installation exposes release, topology, hardware and security decisions before infrastructure preparation. Conditional details cover installation storage, workload dependencies, advanced networking, proxy configuration and custom manifests. The service-access table identifies consumers, destinations, applicability and owners; it is an overview rather than a complete firewall allowlist. Boot-media preparation ends with a brief readiness checkpoint; runtime validation is covered under host installation. Links to the former readiness section open boot-media preparation. New technical topics do not imply new customer evidence.

The overview describes shared bare-metal decisions and tasks. Detailed installation examples follow the OCP 4.22 Agent-based Installer, as explained in About. Installer-specific behavior, configuration, and commands retain their precise terminology and method context. Customer reports retain their original installation-method and evidence labels.

Post-installation begins with a successfully installed cluster and ends with operational preparation and application-team handover. The final stage is **Prepare operations and hand over to application teams**. It covers required Operators, storage, workload networking, image access, identities, monitoring, and operational preparation. Representative workloads verify the agreed requirements using application-team access. Additional components are conditional on workload and site requirements. Update, recovery and support preparation precede final acceptance; platform administrators retain cluster operations. Handover reuses workload validation results and supplies operating information, project request procedures and incident contacts. Storage provisioning and behavior checks share one detail section, with links to the former storage-behavior section redirected there. Existing stage IDs and shared topic links remain usable.

The **Install and configure required Operators** step includes a searchable table of representative additions for storage, networking, virtualization, logging, application recovery, delivery and compliance. Each example identifies the configuration that follows Operator installation. Select additions by workload and site requirements, checking what the installer already supplied. Core Cluster Operators, basic networking and platform monitoring are already part of the base cluster. The examples draw on Red Hat documentation reviewed in November 2026.

Content was reviewed in November 2026 and uses an OCP 4.22 technical baseline. The 21-card collection has eight pre-installation, three installation and ten post-installation records. It adds repeatable configuration, first-VM blockage, identity integration, a Nutanix image-distribution account, migration readiness, support-evidence barriers, operating-team learning and conditional security rollout. It retains prerequisite coordination, platform configuration, diagnosis, recovery and qualified storage/network/access accounts. Installer choice includes a 2026 account with a disputed call date and older field context; ISO usability evidence remains historical. Recovery and continuity requirements, including secret restoration, live in How it works; upgrade accounts appear as lifecycle context there. Physical-execution gaps and unproven formal-handover failures remain in About and the relevant process notes. Reported operating-team learning is represented by a separate, bounded problem card. Preserve the distinction between historical experience, acceptance requirements, adjacent-platform evidence, documented capabilities and hypotheses when editing it. Do not store customer names, source documents, reference registries or citation identifiers in the app.

The automated checks validate complete stage coverage, navigation integrity, problem/opportunity connections, route round-trips, invalid-link recovery and search. Browser checks cover inline disclosure, perspective switching without losing position, linked problems and ideas, refresh/share, history, keyboard navigation and layouts at 320, 390, 768 and 1440 pixels. Human usability testing with engineers can assess whether the information structure helps them learn.

The Journey CSS lint override allows descending specificity because component descendants and responsive rules are grouped together. Other inherited lint rules remain enabled. The bundled DM Sans and Manrope fonts include their SIL Open Font License files in `../../../public/fonts/`.
